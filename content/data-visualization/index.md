Communicating information is at the very core of cartographic expression, yet maps are not the only visual resource type the Center holds. Our collection of historical data graphics presents statistics and other information through many of the same visual techniques as maps, but without relying on geographic space to make a point or tell a story.

The pioneers of data graphics appear throughout this collection. William Playfair invented the line graph and bar chart in the 1780s, “painting to the eye” a century of British and North American trade in one view. In the 1920s, Otto and Marie Neurath, with artist Gerd Arnz, developed the International System of Typographic Picture Education, the Isotype, which uses rows of repeated pictograms to make social and economic statistics visually appealing, memorable, and easy to understand. This visual language spread quickly across Europe and the rest of the world, and is still used in the work of designers today as evident in the graphics that follow.

The Center embraces a wide ranging definition of cartography, stemming from our commitment to enhance learning in visual ways whether or not they are place-based. Historical data graphics expand how we teach deep looking at visual material of every kind, and provide a unique lens through which we can see history in a simple and approachable way.
