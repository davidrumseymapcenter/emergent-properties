Victorian-era ballooning turned the sky into a place of scientific inquiry, and James Glaisher, exploring what he called the “aerial ocean,” worked to demystify its “depths for the causes of atmospheric phenomena.” This plate is a chart, a map, and a view all at once. The balloon’s altitude is plotted minute-by-minute as a line set over a moonlit view of London which appears to float over the streets below. On this particular flight, Glaisher recorded something he felt “remarkable”: the air temperature rose as did the balloon, an inversion of his expectations. *Travels in the Air* contains 125 illustrations of awe-inspiring sights, and serves as an example of data visualization as a thing of beauty, a visual gift presented to readers who would themselves never leave the ground.
