Data storyteller RJ Andrews quotes Otto Neurath in his *Cross Sections Through California*: “The purpose of teaching-pictures is to have an effect on the mind.” The historical styles from this section are alive and working to slice up California into seventeen elevation profiles, their land cover summarized in Isotype-like symbols, explicitly honoring the system Otto and Marie Neurath and Gerd Arntz invented a century ago, such as sans serif lettering, pictograms, and color applied sparingly but with purpose. His choices are as cartographic as they are graphic, rotating north to fit the state into the frame the way we imagine it, valleys and mountains going up and down, with the coast facing out to the Pacific. On the reverse, pictogram charts count crops, electricity, and population since 1850, employing symbols the Isotype pioneers never needed, like solar panels, and honest footnotes, such as that the 1850 census counted only seven thousand women and mostly omitted the state’s Indigenous population.
