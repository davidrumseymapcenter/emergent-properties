In the late 18th century, William Playfair invented many aspects of the visual language we use to communicate with data today. The line graph and bar chart first appeared in his 1786 *Commercial and Political Atlas*, an atlas containing no geographic maps at all, stretching the word to mean a compendium of information presented visually. On this chart, a century of British trade with North America is shown. The gap shaded with green between the pink export and yellow import lines mark the “balance in favour of England,” which pinches abruptly to nothing as the American Revolution halts trade, then rebounds again after peace. In the pages of observations regarding trade with North America, Playfair supported his new visual method as “painting to the eye” to show lines representing quantity and time “with mathematical exactness that is not to be called in question.”
