Kaj Klitgaard uses old cartographic conventions with an artist’s eye. The Whitney Studio Club, founded by sculptor Gertrude Vanderbilt Whitney in 1914, was a haven for Greenwich Village painters and students, and mounted at least ten exhibitions per season featuring unknown artists. This map, commissioned by the Whitney Studio Club, was a map of artists, by artists, for artists. He adds a mermaid and sea monster offshore, includes a scroll cartouche, and renders the neighborhood a miniature model seen from a bird’s-eye view. But he also holds these rules loosely, with depth emphasized by clouds and a magnifying glass hovering over the club itself, allowing the viewer to peer inside. Around the border, portraits of Village artists and verses complete the spirit of the Village’s modern art movement.
