In this piece cartography becomes an artistic language that makes distortion the purpose. Agnes Denes, a leading pioneer of environmental and conceptual art, spent the 1970s asking versions of that question in her series *Isometric Systems in Isotropic Space: Map Projections*, which she completed just before this retrospective exhibition at MIT’s Hayden Gallery. This catalog cover, printed on a graph-paper grid, summarizes the project well. What if Earth were not a sphere? Shown here are six pyramids, three with the continents projected on them, while the others show the globe’s latitude and longitude grid lines stretched over the same unexpected form. Denes projected the world onto pyramids, eggs, snail shells, and hot dogs using the mathematics of map projections, which always distort, to challenge any map’s claim to objectivity.
