This silkscreen-printed, two-part abstracted view of Lake Merritt—a public space at the heart of Oakland’s culture and lifestyle––pays homage to the ways illustrative maps have long told the stories of a place. The print reads in two parts using symbols and patterns to evoke the landscape: the lower half represents the area before European settlers arrived, while the upper half shows the lake as it appears today, surrounded by red buildings, homes, and other industrial symbols. Graham illustrates this local environment much as a cartographer would, using color, space, scale, and the relationships between objects to tell as visual, place-based history. Maps can help us to understand how landscapes have changed over time, just as Graham has done in one glimpse.
