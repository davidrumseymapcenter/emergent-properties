In this large mixed-media piece Connie Zheng maps forty-three of the most notorious toxic sites in the San Francisco Bay Area with oozing, tar-like blobs symbolizing ground poisoned by high-tech manufacturing, military sites, and gold-rush era mining. This is thematic mapping as an artist practices it, through symbolism, juxtaposition, and emotion rather than statistical convention. Its handwritten notes record protests, lawsuits, and remediation efforts by which communities have fought for a healthier environment. Zheng calls this work “a register of the ways in which the Bay Area has been shaped, both figuratively and literally, by dominant settler narratives of the landscape’s potential for wealth and resource extraction.” Is it a land of opportunity, all things considered? The map asks us to weigh the answer.
