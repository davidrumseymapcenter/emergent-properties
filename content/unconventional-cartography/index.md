Mapmaking takes many forms, from artists interpreting their own vision of the world, citizens mapping for social change without regard for cartographic conventions, and practitioners pushing cartography in entirely new directions. This collection is largely unconstrained by traditional definitions of cartography or collectibility, making it a unique corpus of map-ish materials.

These works stretch the imagination of how to see the world. Projections abandon the globe for a pyramid. A neighborhood becomes the center stage. Toxic sites bleed through a landscape like dripping black ink. Each one asks us to think beyond the conventions that the rest of our collections helped establish.

They also open the practice itself. Cartography has traditionally lived inside institutions, with its tools and authority held by surveyors, publishers, and governments. The map makers in this section claim those tools for everyone and treat the map as personal expression, protest, and fun, making cartography an art for all.
