The collections of the David Rumsey Map Center have been shaped by a decade of use and scholarship. As certain materials have resonated with this generation of students, instructors, and researchers, we focus our attention and identify related collections, defining them as distinct clusters within the larger archive. These subsets have in turn defined the David Rumsey Map Center as distinctive, and have set Stanford’s collections apart. As a whole, the collection is vast and not bounded by geography, spanning the 15th century to the present. But within that expanse are specific cohesive areas of strength that have added lasting value to campus and beyond.

These clusters emerge through the ways in which we make collections available. Whether through instruction or supporting research, we promote personal inquiry and encourage our users to make their own discoveries. Every opportunity to look at a map is a chance to uncover a new meaning, find a new story, and add to our understanding about the past. We do not lecture, but instead invite users to think critically. As this kind of scholarship evolves, so does the meaning of these objects, and the ways this collection connects with our community.

In 2026 the David Rumsey Map Center celebrated its tenth year––a moment to reflect on how we have added value to scholarship on campus and around the world. Emergent Properties brings together a selection of highlights from deep within the collections of the David Rumsey Map Center: items that have not only proven to be rare or beautiful, but have become the drivers of scholarship, programming, and support areas that have resonated with our users. The forty objects in this exhibition represent these emergent properties. Though only a glimpse into the full breadth of the Center’s holdings, they reflect what has mattered most over the past ten years.
