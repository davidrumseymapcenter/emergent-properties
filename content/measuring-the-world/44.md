Land had to be inventoried and measured before it could be sold and settled. After statehood in 1850, surveyors divided California using the Public Land Survey System, a rectangular grid with sections called townships measured almost entirely by hand. This map, published in 1854, was the first of eleven issued annually to show the progress and plans for this system to radiate from three initial points at Mount Diablo, San Bernardino, and Mount Pierce, each a prominent peak that could be referenced by surveyors for miles. Faint lines on this map show where townships were completed, proposed, and further subdivided into smaller sections. Only this first edition is oriented with east at the top, showing California as settlers arriving by sea would have seen it. The grid on this map still reflects roads and property lines today.
