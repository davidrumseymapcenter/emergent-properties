Gilbert Thompson, a topographer with the United States Geographical Surveys West of the 100th Meridian, kept this journal on an 1878 expedition ending in Chico, California. Field notebooks like this one were essential for collecting information for survey maps. On the upper page, key information about each location (or “station”) along the route was captured in a preprinted form: distance traveled, compass bearing, and barometer readings for elevation, along with local magnetic declination (“Mag. Var.”), noted in red, and observations like “arriving at Camp 62, a sheep ranch on the Belle Mill Road”. On the lower page, measurements were combined into a carefully hand-rendered map augmented with a sketch of the profile of the horizon across the top. Records like these allowed a route to be retraced and provided rich details for the cartographers responsible for creating final maps for publication.
