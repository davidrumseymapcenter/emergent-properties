Quintants were used on board a ship to aid in wide horizontal angles of hydrographic surveying. Similar to a sextant but with a slightly larger arc—one-fifth rather than one-sixth of a circle—a quintant’s reflecting properties allowed it to measure wide angles up to 144°, enabling maritime surveyors to measure between landmarks on the coast and fix a ship’s position at sea. This pocket-sized tool, in a leather case labeled with “H.O.” for the British Hydrographic Office, is the type of instrument which would have been used for the observed locations in the H.M.S. Swiftshure’s log book: taking measurements at sea level where altitude readings are taken from a true horizon and eliminating the need to correct for an observer’s height above the sea.
