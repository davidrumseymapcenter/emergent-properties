This hand-drawn chart of South America demonstrates how cartographers critiqued, refined, and corrected existing maps as better information became available to them. This map records an unknown cartographer’s efforts to compare the continent’s coastline as shown on existing charts, represented by the thin line off the coast of Brazil, with a new outline informed by fourteen years of improved astronomical observations. Curved red lines in the South Atlantic represent changes in compass variation caused by the Earth’s magnetic field. Today, mariners use sophisticated data models to calculate magnetic declination, or the difference between true north and where the compass points. At the time of this map’s creation, navigators and scientists were working out methods for predicting and mapping this variation using specialized instruments.
