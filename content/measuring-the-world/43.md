David Brunton developed the pocket transit in 1894 as a compact, multi-purpose instrument for field exploration, and partnered with William Ainsworth and Sons soon after to manufacture it. Brunton called it a “transit” and not a standard compass because it does more than point north. This single palm-sized instrument could measure vertical and horizontal angles to a visual target as well as indicate directional bearings, combining a bubble level for aligning the device through gravity with a clinometer showing degrees and percent of slope of the target from the viewer. This specific device is notable because it was made by Brunton in the earliest years of production, as indicated by the serial number 404, retains its working “wet” bubble level, and is known to have once belonged to a Mr. Steed, who worked in the mining industry in Alaska and Argentina.
