Globes were coveted objects as well as educational tools meant to be browsed, turned, and touched. This composite digitally stitches 48 printed segments into 12 curved gores and two polar end caps, the pieces a globe maker would paste onto a sphere, using papier-mâché techniques, to build this 3.5 feet in diameter celestial globe. The Center holds these printed segments as a bound set, just as a globe maker would have received them. Celestial globes were normally made to show the constellations as if viewed from beyond the stars, looking toward the Earth, reversing how we actually see them. Coronelli instead designed this globe to show the heavens as they appear to an observer on Earth: a “concave” sky. Rather than re-engrave his plates, Coronelli achieved this by countproofing, pulling a second impression from a still wet print to reverse the image. This innovation set this globe apart from his contemporaries.
