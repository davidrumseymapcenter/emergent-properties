The oldest map in our collection is based on the work of Claudius Ptolemy, a 2nd century astronomer and mathematician from Alexandria. His popular work, *Geographia*, listed thousands of known places with their coordinates and explained how to project a round world onto a flat map. None of his original manuscripts have survived and it is uncertain if they even contained maps. Latin translations made in Italy in the early 1400s—some of the earliest examples of printed cartography—contained newly constructed maps which were copied and translated into many languages. This map of Italy, from one of several Ptolemaic atlases printed in Germany starting in the 1470s, was printed from a carved woodblock in black ink, and hand-colored in blue, brown, red, and yellow. Rediscovering Ptolemy helped spark the European Age of Exploration, and his underestimation of the Earth's size is thought to have encouraged Columbus's voyage west to the Indies.
