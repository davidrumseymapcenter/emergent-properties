Inspired by the success of Ortelius’s *Theatrum*, Georg Braun and Frans Hogenberg created their own compendium of maps and views of the world’s cities, titled *Civitates Orbis Terrarum* (“Cities of the World”), which was published in six volumes between 1572 and 1617. Hogenberg had engraved most of *Theatrum’s* maps and views, reflecting the close-knit community of mapmakers in northern Europe at the time. This plate from the first volume, devoted mostly to European cities, shows Renaissance Venice with its characteristic canals. It is bordered at the bottom of the page by a numbered list of significant locations, along with a cartouche of contemporary Venetian dress at the center. The composition was based on Jacopo de’ Barbari’s monumental 1500 *View of Venice*, and for centuries this was the standard way of depicting the city, evidence of how cartographers borrowed freely from those before them.
