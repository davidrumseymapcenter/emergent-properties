Louise E. Jefferson, born in Washington, D.C. in 1908, was a prominent African American artist who published educational maps about world cultures for classroom use and was the first African American woman to hold a directorship position in the publishing industry in the United States. This map pays homage to the nation's African American heritage, celebrating prominent figures in politics, science, music, and literature alongside statistics showing the many ways African Americans have shaped the country. Jefferson’s piece is an example of the genre of pictorial maps, which rose in popularity in the 20th century and used colorful illustrations to educate viewers through a more accessible visual language. Recent scholarship in the history of cartography has brought to light many previously unrecognized contributions to this genre made by women like Jefferson.
