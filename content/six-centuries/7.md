Antonio Garcia Cubas, known to many as the father of Mexican geography, made *Atlas Pintoresco É Historico De Los Estados Unidos Mexicanos* (“Picturesque and Historical Atlas of the United States of Mexico”) to convey the identity and character of Mexico. The plates in this atlas celebrate the country’s history, industry, landscape, and people, featuring richly detailed color illustrations made with a printing technique called chromolithography. Published as the nation was redefining itself in the decades after the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo ended the Mexican-American war and ceded its northern territories to the United States, the atlas is an homage to Mexico’s rich and diverse society. This plate mutes national borders, instead highlighting the territories of the country’s distinct ethnic groups, some extending into present-day Arizona and New Mexico, and is framed by vignettes of the peoples and costumes of Mexico’s many regions which reflect hierarchical cultural beliefs of the time.
