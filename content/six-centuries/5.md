A continuous map of the Chinese coast from Manchuria to Vietnam on a hand-colored scroll reads from right to left and is over twenty feet long, although it would have been read by unrolling just one arm’s length at a time. Intended for navigation, the map does not use a uniform scale or grid, and records coastal cities, fortifications, harbors, and reefs along the route. This facsimile section of the scroll shows the area of China and Taiwan, with penciled annotations near the top, where a former owner identified the cities of Zhangzou and Quanzhou in English. Asian cultures developed their own methodologies and practices for depicting geographic space, distinct from those of Europe. Factors like differences in production methods (e.g. printing vs. hand-drawn manuscripts) and collecting practices have resulted in over-representation of European mapmaking traditions in physical archives like this one.
