This hand-colored plate is the first map of the entire African continent to appear in an atlas. Like every map in Ortelius’s *Theatrum Orbis Terrarum* (“Theater of the World”), it is accompanied by descriptive text for a curious European audience, in which Ortelius tells readers that key information about the continent came from an African-born diplomat named Leo Africanus. Notes on the map itself offer additional explanations; a text block in the lower right reads, “This island is called Madagascar by its inhabitants, St. Lawrence by the Spaniards; formerly Menuthias by Ptolemy, perhaps Cerne by Pliny.” Lakes and rivers are simplified and inaccurate, reflecting incomplete European knowledge, while sea monsters and ships in a storm allude to the captivating drama of maritime travel.
