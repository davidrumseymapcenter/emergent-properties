Vandermaelen’s six-volume atlas, *Atlas universel de géographie physique, politique, statistique et minéralogique* (“Universal atlas of physical, political, statistical and mineralogical Geography”), with one volume per inhabited continent, was the first to map the entire world at a uniform scale. In volume three, Africa is divided not into the political or cultural regions Europeans imagined for it, but into standard numbered sections at a constant scale with a consistent set of symbols. The scale allows the maps to be detailed enough to show more precise settlements, river features, and caravan routes than the maps before it. At first, this seems a more objective way to map a continent, but the impression breaks down in the descriptive text. Printed on this sheet in French, it opens with the Portuguese conquest of Angola, inventories the area’s extractable resources, and measures the climate based on what Europeans could endure. The frame of the map was standardized, but only served to emphasize a European point of view.
