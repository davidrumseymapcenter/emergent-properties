Maps of Africa, especially concentrated on South Africa, are a cornerstone of the Center’s holdings, containing thousands of maps and atlases. Nearly all of them were made by Europeans, for European eyes. They map the continent as a coastline to be charted, a mystery to be discovered, and a prize to be claimed. But looking deeply, local places, communities, knowledge, and histories can surface even in maps that were meant to serve an empire.

This collection has grown from notable acquisitions, among them the collections of Dr. Oscar I. Norwich, the Johannesburg collector whose maps of Africa and its islands anchor the holdings, and of Caroline Batchelor. Academic advocates such as Professor Grant Parker have extended its reach by facilitating digital additions from partners including the University of Cape Town. This collection holds few maps of Africa made from African perspectives, a gap in the cartographic record itself, and a priority for future acquisitions into the Center’s holdings.

The maps that follow move from the first atlases of the continent through exploration and empire, and close with a contemporary artist’s vision of an Africa never colonized at all.
