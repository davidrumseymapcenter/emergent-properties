English mapmaker Aaron Arrowsmith drew this chart for a set of five to accompany James Stanier Clark's *The Progress of Maritime Discovery,* a study of early navigational methods and naval history meant for tracing voyages from an armchair and not from a ship at sea. Its title reveals his method: “drawn from various geographical MSS,” or manuscripts, and the mixture shows in place names written in Spanish, Portuguese, English, and Arabic along the coast. Soundings, obstructions, ports, and river mouths dot the shoreline of what is now Morocco and Western Sahara while the interior appears nearly empty, highlighting the utility of this map for understanding maritime routes. The map leaves out one element that would typically be found on maps made for navigation: headland views showing the profile of land from the water used to orient oneself offshore. Their absence is evidence this map wasn’t meant to be used as a practical navigational aid, but instead as a record of the voyages Clark’s book describes.
