The dry Southern California climate limited the growth of major cities until water sources could be developed. William Hammond Hall, designer of Golden Gate Park in San Francisco, and California’s first State Engineer, oversaw early state water projects from flood control to irrigation, and this hand-drawn basin map was his working reference copy and likely the base for the maps in his 1888 report on irrigation in California. Penciled notes indicate a working document, such as the memo at the top: “change mouth of Millard Cañon.” It captures the region during a transformative time, when railroads like the Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe Railway crossed the basin, bringing newcomers drawn to the area’s climate and opportunities. The scattered street grids of separate towns are like islands in the open, dramatically different from the continuous urban landscape we know today.
