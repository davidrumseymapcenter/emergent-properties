The Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo promised that legitimate Mexican land claims would be honored in the new state of California, but in practice landowners had to prove their ownership in court. Under Mexican law, a petition only needed a diseño: a rough sketch describing boundaries by landmarks. American courts demanded more, and the costly surveys, decades-long proceedings, and disputes that followed cost many Californio families—Hispanic Californians who had settled in California under Mexican and Spanish rule—their land and livelihoods. This manuscript plat shows the process’s end stage, with precisely measured boundaries and distances, and was made once the professional surveying industry had established itself in the state. This map confirms ownership of 1,939 acres along the Guadalupe River near San Jose, granted to James Alexander Forbes by Governor Micheltorena in 1844 and later acquired in 1867 by Robert F. Stockton, a central figure in the early American occupation of California.
