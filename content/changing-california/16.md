Driven by hysteria after the bombing of Pearl Harbor, the United States adopted policies that forced Japanese Americans, regardless of whether they were U.S. citizens, into relocation centers. This figure comes from the War Department’s final report on the removal, *Japanese evacuation from the West Coast, 1942 : Final report,* a volume of 43 maps and numerous charts assembled to justify the incarceration as “military necessity.” Using 1940 census data, it extrudes each state to indicate the size of its Japanese American population, making California tower over the rest of the country and fueling the perception that the state needed to be brought under control. It is a reminder that maps do not only record events, but can also be designed to argue for them. In the 1980s it was found that this report had been doctored before reaching the courts, which helped retroactively overturn wartime cases against Japanese Americans for violating curfew and exclusion orders.
