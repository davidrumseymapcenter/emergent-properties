California’s history is usually told as being a Spanish to Mexican to American transition, but this chart is evidence of a third imperial player. In the first half of the 19th century Russian expansion pushed south from Alaska along the coast, mainly drawn by the sea otter fur trade. Fort Ross, just south of the area shown, marked the southernmost Russian settlement in California. Prepared in Sitka, the capital of Russian America, this chart covers the coast from southern Oregon to near Fort Bragg. The three headland views on the right were meant for recognizing landmarks from the water. With an inset of Humboldt Bay, its penciled navigation calculations suggest it might have actually been used aboard a Russian ship. Its title notes that it was "corrected by Colonial mariners," incorporating knowledge gathered on the spot. With imperial priorities in Europe and otter populations collapsing from overhunting, a stronger Russian California never came to be.
