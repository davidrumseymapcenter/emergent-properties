This is the first map of San Francisco made by Chinese Americans for the Chinese community. Chinese immigrants were among the first to arrive in Gold Rush California, and many later built the transcontinental railroad. However they were scapegoated as taking jobs from white laborers, and anti-Chinese sentiment brought violence that escalated to the creation of the Chinese Exclusion Act barring new Chinese laborers from entering the United States in 1882. Exclusion spurred stronger community institutions like the Chinese Consolidated Benevolent Association, for whom this map was made. Written almost entirely in Chinese, it closely resembles a map the City of San Francisco made in 1885 to investigate Chinatown’s living conditions. Four decades later, that outsider’s survey is reborn as an insider’s portrait of a cohesive neighborhood, with an inset map showing ferry connections to Oakland’s Chinatown across the Bay.
