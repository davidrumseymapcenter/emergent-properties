Regarded as the first printed map to show California depicted as an island, this map accompanied an article by the English mathematician Henry Briggs in Samuel Purchas’s voyage collection, *Purchas his Pilgrimes.* The idea had roots in a popular Spanish literary work from around 1500 which described an island inhabited solely by women and where gold was the only existing metal. In around 1620 the Dutch captured a document describing California as an island from a Spanish ship. Briggs learned of this and felt confident enough to use it as a source for this map, as the note at lower left explains. This map is part of the Center’s Glen McLaughlin Collection, the largest collection of maps depicting California as an Island, with maps dating from 1622 to 1865. These depictions blend fantasy and reality and invite us to ask how this error persisted so long after the true nature of California was known.
