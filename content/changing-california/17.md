The War Department’s report mapped Japanese Americans as a problem to be managed, but this map was made by the incarcerated themselves. At Tule Lake, in the far northeastern corner of California near the border with Oregon, more than 7,000 people were incarcerated. This simple map, printed on legal-sized paper with the few resources at hand, was issued by the Tulean Dispatch, a newspaper published to keep residents of the camp informed of events, announcements, and news from other camps. Its numbered blocks map where people lived, while its drawings show how they made the camp as much like home as they could: a couple holding hands, baseball players, a bicyclist, the school, hospital, and post office. A former resident annotated this copy by hand, marking friends’ barracks and, beside Block 54, the words “I live here.”
