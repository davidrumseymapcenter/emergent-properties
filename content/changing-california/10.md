The Gold Rush transformed California from a military frontier territory into an international cultural and economic center, and maps like this one helped initiate this profound change. Regarded as the first accurate map of gold discoveries in the state, it was published with President Polk’s December 5, 1848 message to Congress which, alongside testimonial letters from Californians, helped turn the rumor of gold into a reality. Among that evidence was Thomas O. Larkin’s letter written six months after the discovery at Sutter’s Mill: “I do not think I am exaggerating in estimating the amount of gold obtained on the rivers… at ten thousands dollars a day for the last few days.” On the map, the discoveries appear between Sutter’s Fort and Mountain Lake, now Lake Tahoe, at the site labeled “Lower Mine.” Prompted by the news, thousands of migrants poured into the state the following year, changing California forever.
