The Center’s collections trace California’s evolution through maps, from the lands of Indigenous peoples, through Spanish and Mexican Alta California, to its incorporation into the United States. These cartographic records chronicle the complex histories and distinctive cultures that persist in California today.

California is deeply integrated into the Center’s holdings. Maps of the state where he lived were among David Rumsey’s earliest collecting interests, and a large portion of our California material comes from his collection. More recently, Stanford University Libraries and the Bill Lane Center for the American West jointly acquired the collections of the California Historical Society, bringing more than 2,900 historical maps of the state to the Center. Because Stanford’s curricula has a distinctly Californian flavor, these materials lend themselves naturally to instruction and engagement and complement the Libraries’ many other California collections, from photographs to music to manuscripts.

The maps that follow show California in constant change. An island invented by European imagination, a gold rush that remade the state almost overnight, borders and land ownership redrawn by war and law, and the communities who mapped their own place in the state that has continuously reinvented itself.
