The year after the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo ended the Mexican-American War and transferred the Southwest to the United States, this pocket map contextualized the newest land acquisitions in the shifting borders of the vast American West. Secluded on the coast, Upper California already hints at its future stature. The “Gold Regions,” highlighted in faded yellow ink, stand out as the territory’s major feature. One of its two insets charts the maritime and overland routes from the populated eastern seaboard, journeys that could run as long as the 17,000 mile passage around Cape Horn at the southern tip of South America. The other details the gold and quicksilver district itself. Folded into leather covers, it was made to travel west in a forty-niner’s pocket.
